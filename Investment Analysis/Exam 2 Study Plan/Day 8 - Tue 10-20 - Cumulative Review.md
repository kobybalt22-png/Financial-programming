## Part 2: Mixed problem set (30 min, no notes)

The problems are deliberately out of order, which is how they'll show up on the exam.

**1.** A 2-year, 10% **annual**-coupon bond has a YTM of 8%. Find its **(a)** price, **(b)** Macaulay duration, **(c)** modified duration, and **(d)** the estimated % price change if the yield rises 100 bps.

<details><summary>Show answer</summary>

(a) 100/1.08 + 1,100/1.08² = 92.59 + 943.07 = **\$1,035.67**
(b) Weights: 92.59/1,035.67 = 0.0894; 943.07/1,035.67 = 0.9106. D_Mac = 1(0.0894) + 2(0.9106) = **1.9106 years**
(c) D_Mod = 1.9106 / 1.08 = **1.7691**
(d) ≈ −1.7691 × 0.01 = **−1.77%**
</details>

**2.** The 2-year zero-coupon yield is 5.5% and the 3-year zero-coupon yield is 6%. Find the forward rate for year 3. Under expectations theory, what does it represent?

<details><summary>Show answer</summary>

f₃ = 1.06³ / 1.055² − 1 = **7.01%**. It's the market's expected 1-year rate in year 3.
</details>

**3.** A 15-year, 4% semiannual-coupon bond has a YTM of 5.5%. Find its price. Premium or discount?

<details><summary>Show answer</summary>

N = 30, I/Y = 2.75, PMT = 20, FV = 1,000, so **\$848.13**. Discount (4% < 5.5%).
</details>

**4.** A 10-year, 9% annual-coupon bond has a YTM of 8%. Find its price and current yield, and write the coupon/CY/YTM ordering.

<details><summary>Show answer</summary>

N = 10, I/Y = 8, PMT = 90, FV = 1,000, so **\$1,067.10**. CY = 90/1,067.10 = **8.43%**
Premium: 9% > 8.43% > 8% ✔
</details>

**5.** You buy a 10-year, 7% annual-coupon bond at par and hold it 3 years. You reinvest coupons at 5%, and the bond's YTM is 6% when you sell. Find the annual RCY.

<details><summary>Show answer</summary>

1. Sale price (7 years left): N = 7, I/Y = 6, PMT = 70, FV = 1,000, so **\$1,055.82**
2. FV of coupons: N = 3, I/Y = 5, PMT = 70, so **\$220.68**
3. Terminal wealth = **\$1,276.50**
4. RCY = (1,276.50/1,000)^(1/3) − 1 = **8.48%**
</details>

**6.** *(Concept, rapid fire)*
- (a) Which has higher duration: a 10-year zero, or a 10-year 8% coupon bond?
- (b) Yields fall 300 bps. Does duration over- or under-estimate the price gain?
- (c) Corporate yield = 7%, Treasury = 4.5%. What's the spread in bps?
- (d) A premium callable bond: is YTC usually above or below YTM?
- (e) The yield curve is inverted. What does expectations theory say?

</details>
